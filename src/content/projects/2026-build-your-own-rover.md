I created a platform for visitors to make their own rover and test it out in our roverscape. I made a standardized box that visitors could attach different wheel designs to and press a button to turn it on. 

![Photo of workbench](/images/projects/IMG_20250727_180614.jpg "Workbench Production Line")

Dwell time was very high and visitors loved the prototyping process and seeing which wheels would preform better than others. 

These rovers were made in conjunction with three "test beds". One was filled with sand, another with gravel and one with a mix. In addition, the activity took place in our existing Roverscape playground and there were plenty of visitors roaming around seeing how their rover did "in-situ". 

The team and I came up with different shapes we could make on the laser cutter that added a lot to the iterative experience. There was a lot of surprise on the circles not working very well!

![](/images/projects/IMG_20250802_113307.jpg)![](/images/projects/IMG_20250730_122213.jpg)

This was a really quick project and a good demonstration of the ethos of our Executive Director at the time. We were focused on incredibly quick turnaround times (this from design to implementation was \~2 weeks), and less focused on fit and finish. It helped I facilitated the exhibit the whole time, but I really got to see how engaging this was for visitors from \~3 all the way to \~90. The activity was a easy way to give an excuse to make something. I loved seeing parents start parallel playing alongside their kids - just as focused on making their rover.
